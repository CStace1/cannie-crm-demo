Benchmark, candidate record

The candidate record against the same ten systems

Twelve things a UK agency expects a candidate record to hold. Ten systems scored, plus the prototype's candidate record.

19 September 2026. Nothing tested hands-on. Bullhorn, Vincere and Firefish read from their own help centres; the rest from product pages and 2026 comparison guides.

The twelve expectations

  1. Profile and contact. Name, routes, location, current role and employer, owner, source, status (active, passive, placed, do not contact).
  2. CV parsed, versioned. Upload parses into fields; every version kept; the sent version recorded per job.
  3. Skills and experience. Structured skills with years or level, work history, qualifications; searchable.
  4. Live applications across jobs. Every job they are on, stage and days, in one list; move from here as well as from the job.
  5. Availability, notice and pay. Notice, available from, current and wanted salary or rate, engagement type; history of asks over time.
  6. Right to work and compliance. Document, expiry, checked by and when; IR35 determinations; DBS, references, qualifications with expiry; expiring items surface.
  7. Consent and retention. Lawful basis, consent date and source, marketing preferences, retention date, erasure request; the record answers a subject access request.
  8. Placements and timesheets. Every placement with dates, client, rate, fee or margin; timesheet history and outstanding.
  9. Communications log. Emails, calls, SMS, meetings in one stream, with templates and two-way sync.
  10. Matching from the candidate. Jobs that fit this person, surfaced without a separate search.
  11. Duplicates. Detected on create and on parse, with merge.
  12. Portal or self-service. Candidate updates own details, availability, documents, timesheets.

What the field agrees on

Ten of ten: profile, parsed CV, structured skills, applications across jobs, consent, comms log, duplicate detection. Nine of ten match jobs to the candidate. The candidate record is the most mature entity in the category; nobody is behind on the basics. The spread is on three things: compliance depth, pay history, and a portal.

Vincere and Firefish carry full compliance: document types with expiry, checked by, reminders; IR35 status on the candidate and the placement; DBS and references as tracked items. Both have a candidate portal for availability, documents and timesheets. Firefish's GDPR module is the clearest: lawful basis per candidate, consent capture from the job board application, retention rules with an erasure queue.

Bullhorn has the strongest placement history (every placement, with rates and the correlated job fields) and comms (email and SMS synced, with templates), but compliance is through Onboarding365 and partners, not the record.

Access and Tracker carry timesheets on the candidate because the back office is theirs. Access is the only one showing pay history as a timeline.

Recruit CRM, Manatal, Zoho, Loxo are perm-shaped: strong parsing and enrichment (LinkedIn, sourcing extensions), AI scoring, kanban of applications. Compliance is a custom field. Rate history does not exist as a concept. Portal is absent or a job board only.

Why the prototype scores what it scores

Scored on Candidate Record.dc.html. Eleven of twelve; level with Vincere and Firefish, ahead of the rest on 5, 6 and 8.

1 to 3: Yes. Header carries name, role, location, owner, source, routes and status. CV versions kept, the one sent recorded per application, upload parses into changes you confirm before they touch the profile. Skills with level and years, work history, qualifications.

4, Applications: Yes. Every job with stage, days and next step; a stage move with a required note from the candidate side writes to the job. Interviews and offers still book on the job because they need the client's diary and terms.

5, Availability and pay: Yes. Notice, available from, engagement, current and wanted, plus every ask and agreed figure since 2020 as one timeline. Screens, offers and placements write to it.

6, Compliance: Yes. Documents with state, expiry, checked by and how; rechecks logged; IR35 determination per placement with the SDS on file; references named and taken at offer. Expiries feed Needs you.

7, Consent: Yes. Lawful basis, date, source, marketing, retention and review; subject access and erasure recorded as requests with the clock started. Nothing deletes.

8, Placements: Yes. Perm and contract placements with agreed figure, fee or margin, rebate outcome; timesheet history with outstanding first.

9 to 11: Yes. One activity stream with kind filters, email and SMS sent from templates and logged (sync itself seeded, not connected, as on the client). Matches from the candidate with one action. Duplicates flagged on create and parse, merge keeps both histories.

12, Portal: No. Noted, not designed.

Where that leaves it

The opening is the contractor as a person over time, not the applicant on one job.

Every system holds the perm applicant well. Only Vincere, Firefish and Access hold the contractor: pay history, IR35 across placements, documents that expire, availability that returns. Cannie is a perm, SOW and T&M shop, so the candidate record should read as a working history: what they have asked for and been paid, where they have been placed, what paper is in date, when they are free next. The job record already captures most of this at screen time; the candidate record is where it accumulates.

What the candidate record must carry

  1. Header: name, current role and employer, location, status chip (active, placed, on assignment, passive, do not contact), owner, source, routes, Needs you (expiring paper, stalled application, ending assignment, availability date reached, consent review due).
  2. Five facts: Live applications, Wants (latest ask), Available, Right to work (state and expiry), Consent (basis and review date).
  3. Applications tab first: every job with stage, days, next step, the same row and drawer as the job record; move, book, note from here. Closed applications below with outcome.
  4. Profile tab: CV versions (parsed fields, which version went to which job), skills with level and years, work history, qualifications, the three descriptions of the person (the CV, your summary, the public profile for shortlists).
  5. Availability and pay tab: notice, available from, engagement, current and wanted as a timeline of asks and agreed figures across applications and placements.
  6. Compliance tab: documents with kind, expiry, checked by and when, version; IR35 determinations per placement; DBS and references. Expiries feed Needs you on candidate, job and client.
  7. Placements tab: every placement with client, job, dates, rate or fee, margin, rebate outcome; timesheets outstanding and history.
  8. Consent tab: lawful basis, consent date and source, marketing preferences, retention date, subject access and erasure requests with dates. Nothing deletes; erasure is a state.
  9. Activity: the one stream, with the job and client each entry belongs to; email and SMS seeded as on the client.
  10. Matches: jobs on the desk that fit, one action, add to pipeline at Screened.
  11. Duplicates: flagged on create and parse by email, phone, name and employer; merge keeps both histories.
  12. Portal: noted, not designed.
