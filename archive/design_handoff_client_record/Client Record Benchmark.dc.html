Benchmark, client record

The client record against ten systems on the market

Twelve things a UK recruitment agency expects a client record to hold, taken from what the incumbents already do, not from Cannie's own brief. Ten systems and the prototype scored on the same twelve.

15 September 2026. Nothing tested hands-on. Four systems read from their own help centres (the repo's research note), six from product pages and recent UK comparison guides. Confidence is stated per row.

The twelve expectations

Drawn from the research note's table of what is standard across Bullhorn, Vincere, Firefish and JobAdder, plus the four things only some carry that a buyer still asks about. Each is a question, so the prototype can be asked it too.

  1. Company profile and status. Registration, VAT, addresses, size, sector, and a status or stage (prospect, client, partner, lapsed).
  2. Contacts with roles and consent. Named people, what they do for you (approver, invoice recipient, hiring manager), and their marketing or email consent.
  3. Jobs on the record. The client's open and closed jobs with pipeline status, from the client page.
  4. Notes and tasks with dates. Notes with a follow-up date, tasks filterable by type, an owner.
  5. Communications history. Email, and usually SMS, logged against the record automatically, not retyped.
  6. Placement history. Who has been placed, when, on what terms.
  7. Timesheets on the record. Time entered against this client's placements, visible from the client.
  8. Terms, rates and fees. Negotiated perm fees and contract rate bands, held on the client and applied downstream.
  9. Invoices and billing. Billing contact, invoice history, outstanding and overdue, on the client.
  10. Documents and compliance. Signed terms, credit check, compliance paperwork, with expiry.
  11. Company hierarchy. Parent and child companies, brands, sites, billing entities.
  12. Custom fields. Agency-defined fields on top of the standard set.

Why the prototype scores what it scores

1, Profile: Part. Registration, VAT, typed addresses and sector are there. Status has Prospect, Active, Dormant, Do not work with. Missing the steps between prospect and client (engaged, terms sent, terms signed) that Vincere and Firefish name, and any leads or opportunities list before the first job.

2, Contacts: Part. Roles are stronger than most (hiring manager, approves time, approves statements, receives invoices). No marketing or email consent field, no preferred contact method, no left date. The repo's own contact design names all three.

3, Jobs: Yes. Open and filled, pipeline counts, manager, next step, full list with search. Ahead of the field on presentation.

4, Notes and tasks: Yes. Typed notes with kind, who, about which job, follow up; next actions with owner and date; done moves to activity.

5, Communications: Part. Teams meetings with transcript attach or summary is ahead of anyone. But there is no email or SMS sync onto the activity, and every incumbent has it. This is the biggest daily-use gap.

6, Placements: Yes. Filled and live with started, runs to, rebate window, end date. A compliance roster per contractor on People.

7, Timesheets: Part. Exceptions only (time awaiting approval) under Billing and in Needs you. No list of time by placement on the record. Bullhorn, Vincere, Access and Tracker all show a timesheets tab on the company.

8, Terms and rates: Yes. Editable rate card with bands, charge, pay, margin, applied at the work date; fees by job type; payment terms; supplier position; review dates. Level with the pay and bill systems, ahead of the challengers.

9, Billing: Part. Invoiced, outstanding, overdue, debtor days, and a short invoice list. No billing contact designation beyond a contact role, no credit limit, no full ledger. The incumbents with pay and bill show all of it.

10, Documents and compliance: Part. Versioned, nothing deleted, contracts split from compliance, expiry in the state column. No credit check row (Firefish, Vincere hold one), no e-signature or signed status feed. Versioning as built is ahead of anything found.

11, Hierarchy: Part. Typed addresses (head office, site, invoicing) and a billing entity name. No parent or child company, no brands or divisions. Firefish and Vincere model it; a group with several billing entities is common in IT recruitment.

12, Custom fields: No. Every one of the ten offers agency-defined fields on the client. The prototype has "Other" on several pick lists, which is not the same thing. A buyer migrating from any of these will ask on day one.

Where that leaves it

Ahead on how the record reads, behind on what it holds.

On the twelve things the industry expects, the prototype is Yes on four, Part on seven, No on one. Vincere is Yes on all twelve; Bullhorn, Firefish, Access and Tracker on nine or ten. The gap is not design, it is plumbing that has not been modelled yet: email sync, timesheets on the record, credit check, hierarchy, custom fields. The things the prototype does that nobody else does (jobs split to fill and filled, statements of work with milestones and burn, a derived needs you line, a compliance roster per client, versioned paper, a record-scoped search) are real, and they are not on this list because no one else expects them yet.

What to close first

  1. Email onto the activity. Two way sync from Outlook or Google. Without it the activity log is what people remember to type, and every incumbent has made that unacceptable.
  2. Company status with stages. Prospect, engaged, terms sent, terms signed, client, dormant. One field, and it unlocks a leads view before the first job.
  3. Timesheets on the record. A list by placement and period under Billing, not only the exceptions.
  4. Contact consent and preferred route. Three fields from the repo's own contact design.
  5. Credit check row. One line under Compliance with a date and a limit.
  6. Parent and child companies. Then billing entities per child.
  7. Custom fields. Last, because they are a migration promise more than a design, but a buyer will ask.
